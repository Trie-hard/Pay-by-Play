PAY -BY- PLAY

Does pay follow play, and does the pay structure change the answer?

A cross-league econometrics and decision-support layer for women's professional sports leagues (PWHL hockey, WNBA basketball, and WPL cricket).

01 / Foundation

Brand Purpose & Modernist Geometry

Pay-by-Play exists at the intersection of labor econometrics and athletic performance. The visual identity is anchored in the principles of De Stijl (The Style) — pioneered by Piet Mondrian and Gerrit Rietveld — which uses asymmetric rectilinear grids, pure primary colors, and bold structural framing to convey mathematical honesty and structural integrity.

METRIC MAPPING 01
⚖️

Pay vs. Play

Pay (Blue) represents the financial baseline, disclosed salary bands, and cap constraints. Play (Red) represents on-field box score production and game-level value.

THE VALUE GAP 02
📐

The Surplus Signal

Canary Yellow is reserved for the gap metric (value_pct − pay_pct). It signals surplus value, confident bargains, and players whose production outruns their paycheck.

ATHLETIC GRID 03
🏒

Pitch & Court Lines

Structural ink lines echo both financial data tables and sports playing surfaces: the hockey goal crease, the basketball key, and the cricket batting crease.

Palette Hierarchy & Distribution Rule

In any application, maintain the 40-30-15-10-5 balance: 40% Paper White (open breathing room), 30% Structural Ink (data lines, text, framing), 15% De Stijl Red (action callouts, highlights), 10% De Stijl Blue (foundational anchors, payroll bars), and 5% Canary Yellow (focal points, value gap badges, bargains).

05 / Governance

Brand Rules: Do’s and Don’ts

To maintain visual recognition and authoritative econometric presentation, adhere to these guidelines.

Status Guideline Rationale
✓ DO Always include the -BY- connector in the complete mark. Reinforces the trademark name and the relationship between compensation and athletic output.
✓ DO Maintain the 1:1 square aspect ratio on app icons. Preserves the balanced Mondrian rectilinear cell distribution.
✓ DO Keep the Play triangle pointing directly forward (to the right). Represents forward kinetic progress and playback of sports action.
✗ DON'T Never replace primary colors with gradients or pastels. De Stijl requires pure, unadulterated primary colors and stark black structural lines.
✗ DON'T Never remove the outer structural black border. The outer border contains the geometry and prevents bleeding on light surfaces.
✗ DON'T Never rotate or skew the logo mark or typography. All lines must remain purely orthogonal (horizontal and vertical).
